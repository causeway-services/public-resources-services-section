# TRANSIT LAYER v0.1 — sewn apparel construction model

**Source and status:** a first **non-knit, cut-and-sew** original garment-model experiment under Causeway Public Consumer Resources, 18 October 2026. **Prototype pattern pieces, not an accepted pattern, retail product, tested fit, or printer/cutter release.** This is separate from Lenticule Knitting Science's CITY LOOPS knitted garment products and from Fine Print Mercantile graphic POD merchandise. The source signal is the already-researched 2022–23 *oversized city layering / clean-fit* direction in [CITY LOOPS's attributed research](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/engineering-science-services-section/services/fibercraft-services-section/services/knitting-science-services-section/research/2026-10-12-city-loops-urban-china-knitwear-research.md), translated to a **new plain-woven garment**, not a copy of a designer, trend photo, indigenous textile or proprietary pattern. Actual market appetite is **unknown**.

## Product and independent wearer question

A simple sleeveless, collarless, open-front woven layering vest with gentle waist-independent volume. Does the drape, open V, clean shoulder seam and armhole provide attractive everyday layering over a T-shirt or collared shirt, without bunching under arm motion? The first comparison is a traced muslin on a **single example body**. The body chest 1000 mm is a *hypothetical measurement fixture* (not an actual person's body data); nominal circumference-equivalent panel sum 1200 mm yields an exploratory +200 mm ease **only if front edges meet**, which they need not. Final fit, wearing ease, coverage and closure are not implied.

## Model's actual files and intended consumers

- `transit-layer-v01.svg`: 1:1 **1620 × 790 mm** vector layout of solid **CUT** outlines, dashed **SEAM** lines, grain arrows and a 100 × 100 mm scale square. A patternmaker or cutter can examine the editable real geometry, then print/plot with no automatic fit-to-page scaling. It is a *layout visualization*, not a material-saving nesting/marker plan.
- `transit-layer-v01.dxf`: plain AutoCAD AC1015 DXF in **millimetres**; `CUT`, `SEAM`, `GRAIN`, `NOTE` layers, closed polylines with corresponding coordinates. It is **not** ASTM D6673/DXF-AAMA-compatible by claim: legacy [ASTM D6673-10 was withdrawn in 2019](https://store.astm.org/d6673-10.html). Confirm target CAD import and unit interpretation. Manufacturer cutter instructions and marker/nesting remain absent.
- Both projections come from the same **three described piece polygons**, but no persistent new CAD generator was installed. Changes must be measured and reconciled in both source files through the original approved Red Cup engineering/fabrication route when a reusable producer is specified. A check of closed polygons and matching seam lengths is a geometric check, not an observed garment.

## Geometry (declared in millimetres at the seam line, excluding 10 mm allowance)

Three pieces: one **full BACK** panel, one **RIGHT FRONT**, one **mirrored LEFT FRONT** (not a back-on-fold half that might be miscut). The nominal back width at side lines is **600 mm**, each front **300 mm**; all hem seam lines y=650 mm. Matching shoulder joining edges (from neck-side x=90 to shoulder x=260, y=0→8) each measure **170.1881 mm**. Matching front/back side joining lines from y=250 to hem y=650 each measure **400 mm**. The back neckline curves below the shoulder line by **38 mm**; the opening on each front reaches **235 mm** down at center front. Edges around armhole are not joined to sleeves; they are independently finished. Each `CUT` polygon is an approximately **10 mm exterior miter offset** of its `SEAM` polygon. The miter is a digital construction assumption: check printed distance and cut/fit tolerance, especially concave armhole segments and V-neck.

Three pieces represent one sewn sample; no dart, pocket, collar, sleeve, closure, lining, center-back seam or additional size is specified. Grain arrows align vertically with center-front/back length, not an asserted optimized marker layout. A 100-mm square rejects accidental inch↔mm and fit-to-paper scaling.

## Original sample construction and bill of materials (hypotheses to check)

- Trial fabric: **prewashed plain-woven cotton** in the approximate 130–180 g/m² class, stable enough to reveal pattern and seam errors without stretchy knit compensation. Select actual material/lot and wash/care procedure before cutting; check shrinkage first. No fiber-specific care claim is made without that material.
- Test yardage: provision **~1.1 m of 150 cm-wide woven cloth plus up to ~6 m of compatible bias binding** or a separately accepted equivalent facing/edge-finish approach; these are **planning allowances**, not verified marker yield or final consumption.
- Identify right/wrong fabric sides and grain; mark cut outline, seam line, center-front, neck, shoulder/side join endpoints and armscye. Cut **one back**, **one left front**, **one right front**, verifying mirroring, grain and 100-mm square. Flat sew the two 170.19-mm shoulder seamline pairs and two 400-mm side pairs at the 10-mm seam line. Finish armholes, center fronts/V-neck and bottom hems using an independently chosen binding/facing/hem operation compatible with the cut allowance; **do not improvise omitted construction into a production approval**. Press and record changes.
- Record physically cut dimensions vs CAD, seam alignment mismatch, fabric mass/width, slip/fraying, armhole/collarbone/shoulder fit, coverage, arm-forward motion, behavior over a shirt, wash shrinkage, skin feel and any necessary seam/edge revisions. Photograph the wearer only with consent. The first actual muslin is the essential next receiver; reject the block rather than automatically grade it if fit is poor.

## Market, standards and rights boundary

Compare with [Seamly2D](https://seamly.io/) as a measurement-variable pattern drafting baseline; [CLO DXF import/export](https://support.clo3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/115000493067-2D-Pattern-DXF-Import-Export) for units, notches and interchange; and [Browzwear Tech Pack](https://help.browzwear.com/en/articles/13065820-editing-tech-pack-properties) for proper multi-size BOM, seam allowance, art placement and DXF export capability. We have not met those mature CAD/tech-pack features. Images/research are **inspiration and evidence**, not production rights or grounds to copy another garment. No pricing or sale is authorized here.

**Science** [Fibercraft](https://github.com/lenticule-science/science-services-section/blob/main/services/engineering-science-services-section/services/fibercraft-services-section/README.md) owns garment mechanics and empirical tests. **Causeway Public Consumer Resources** holds this *instance design* and selects its wearer/customer, garment rights and later merchandising; it does not own a competing software compiler. **Red Cup Engineering** must supply any **reusable** pattern CAD/grade/exporter under its accepted full software-fabrication stages; this particular vector candidate does not assert such a generator exists. **Coesite Operations** must actually obtain a cut/sewn muslin from a capable operator before the Group can receive any fit result. **561 Group** independently receives the outcome; **Causeway** can select shop/public distribution only with rights, multi-size validation, accurate samples, acceptable unit economics and original channel authority. Link any resulting product to [shop's existing multi-seller, physical-goods extension Work #17](https://github.com/causeway-services/public-services-section/issues/17) without silently adding it to the live **books-only** catalog.

**Negative/alternate cases:** an inches-as-millimetres importer, mispaired shoulder/side seam, missing opposite front panel, untested size grade, and an unlicensed garment imitation must all be refused before production. In particular, three correct CAD pieces cannot qualify another body size, a 3D fit simulation is not a sample, and a physically sewn mockup is not proof of market demand.
